Learn by Doing

Fun Scout Skills

Simple field techniques carried over from the original Troop 199 site, now with clear instructions and practical cautions.

Navigation

Find true north without a compass

Place a straight stick vertically in level ground and mark the tip of its shadow. Wait 15–20 minutes and mark the new tip. A line from the first mark to the second runs approximately west to east; the first mark is west. Stand with west on your left and east on your right to face north.

This is approximate. Carry a map and compass, and never rely on one improvised method for critical navigation.

Weather

Estimate lightning distance

Count the seconds between seeing lightning and hearing thunder. Divide by five for an approximate distance in miles, or divide by three for kilometers.

If you hear thunder, lightning is close enough to strike. Seek a substantial building or hard-topped vehicle immediately; never delay shelter to finish counting.

Daylight

Estimate remaining daylight by hand

With your arm fully extended, place your hand between the sun and horizon. Each finger width represents roughly 15 minutes; four stacked fingers represent about one hour. Stack hands downward if needed.

Never look directly at the sun. Terrain, latitude, season, and hand size make this only a rough planning estimate.

Observation

Measure angles in the sky

At arm’s length, your little finger is about 1°, three middle fingers about 5°, a closed fist about 10°, and an outstretched hand from thumb to little finger about 20–25°. Use these references to estimate angular separation.

Calibrate your own hand against known angles for better estimates; everyone’s proportions differ.

Core Outdoor Skills

What Scouts practice

Navigation

Map symbols, compass bearings, route planning, terrain awareness, and what to do when separated from the group.

Camping

Site selection, tents and knots, sleep systems, weather readiness, sanitation, and safe camp routines.

Cooking

Balanced menus, safe food handling, stove safety, patrol cooking, cleanup, and responsible waste management.

First Aid

Recognizing hazards, getting help, and providing age-appropriate first aid within current BSA training.
